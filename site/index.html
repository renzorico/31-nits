The 31-night line

In Catalonia, a tourist stay is 31 days or fewer. Where do Airbnb listings' minimum stays sit against that line? A minimum stay is a setting hosts choose, not an actual stay or a legal finding.

Method and limits

The label marks the Catalan tourist-stay limit. “Draw your own line” shows how many listings sit on each side of any number of nights; its dashed line starts at the limit.

Why 31? A tourist stay is “a continuous period of 31 days or fewer” (Decret 75/2020, art. 221-1). Whether a 31-night booking counts as 31 days or fewer is ambiguous, so listings asking exactly 31 are shown on their own. A minimum stay is what a host asks, not how long guests stay or how a stay is classified in law. Other regions draw their lines differently, so across cities 31+ is only a common measurement threshold.

Is Barcelona different?

Month-plus listings (31+ nights) were more likely than shorter-stay ones to be absent from the later scrape in Barcelona. Catalonia extended its rent limit to seasonal lets on 1 January 2026. The check: the same gap in cities where that law does not apply, with uncertainty that allows for one operator withdrawing many listings at once.

Licences on paper, listings online

Barcelona's official register of tourist flats against Airbnb listings.

Barcelona publishes its register of tourist flats (HUT) every quarter. It is the legal count of licences, which Airbnb listings are not.

One dot per neighbourhood, September 2026. Dots above the diagonal have more listings with open dates than registered flats. Colour shows the share of listings asking 31+ nights. Grey dots have no listings.

Method and limits

Units differ on purpose: the register counts records, the matching counts licence numbers, and the Airbnb side counts listings. A match shows that a number exists in the register; it does not show that the listing is that flat, and it cannot show how many licensed flats are absent from Airbnb (they may let elsewhere, or not at all). Each snapshot is compared with the register quarter nearest in time; the June snapshot uses Q1 because Q2 is not published. A minimum stay is a setting, not proof of how a flat is let.

The average monthly rent of new leases, from the deposits landlords lodge with the Catalan housing agency. Dashed lines mark when the rent limit and its extension to seasonal lets started.

The timing is shown; the cause is not. Interest rates, wages, supply and the mix of flats rented all move the average. The vertical axis of the first view starts at €900, not zero, which makes the swings look larger than they are.
